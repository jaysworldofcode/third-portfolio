Motion is one of the fastest ways to make a brand feel alive, and one of the fastest ways to make a website feel slow. The difference comes down to purpose.

## Every animation needs a job

Before we animate anything, we ask what it's for. Good answers sound like:

- **Orientation:** showing where something came from or where it went.
- **Feedback:** confirming that a click, tap or form submission worked.
- **Personality:** a signature moment, like a logo reveal, that people remember.

"It looks cool" is allowed once per page. Not more.

## Keep it quick

Most interface animations should finish in **200 to 500 milliseconds**. Longer pieces, like a page loader or a hero reveal, can stretch a little further, but they should never block someone who wants to get on with their visit.

## Use one easing family

Mixing a dozen easing curves makes motion feel random. We pick one or two (usually a strong ease-out) and use them everywhere, so the whole brand moves with the same rhythm.

## Respect reduced motion

Some people get dizzy or distracted by movement, and their device tells us so through the *prefers-reduced-motion* setting. When it's on, we swap slides and zooms for simple fades, or skip the animation entirely. Nobody should have to choose between your brand and feeling well.

## Motion beyond the screen

The same principles carry into launch films and social content. A consistent rhythm, a signature transition and a recognisable easing make even a six-second clip feel unmistakably yours.
